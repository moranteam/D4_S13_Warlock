### Respec Event Summaries

**Respec 1 (Level 15):** The Mastermind Shard class quest becomes available, granting Summon Laalish. The Encircling Terror upgrade for Dread Claws also unlocks, fundamentally changing how Dread Claws interacts with your Greater Demon by extending the AoE ring to both you and the demon simultaneously. This respec shifts the build from basic Dread Claws spam to the full Shadowform-stacking identity that defines the endgame.[1][3][4]

**Respec 2 (Level 30):** A mid-leveling respec to consolidate passive nodes and reset any early filler points that were spent in sub-optimal passive clusters. The exact passive trees affected depend on individual progression but the goal is to align the passive constellation with the Demonology and Abyss damage nodes that scale Dread Claws most efficiently.[5][4]

**Respec 3 (Level 34):** The Nether Step Recall Shadows upgrade becomes available. This is the QoL-defining moment of the build ,  Recall Shadows semi-automates repositioning so the player no longer has to manually track positioning constantly. This respec also finalizes the active skill bar layout ahead of the Metamorphosis unlock.[4]

**Respec 4 (Level 40):** Final pre-ultimate cleanup respec. Removes any remaining placeholder skills or passive filler and sets up the exact skill bar state ready to slot Terror Demon Metamorphosis at level 41. Post-respec 4 skill state becomes the endgame leveling target that carries through to level 70.[5][4]

## Appendix: Key Build Mechanics Reference

### Shadowform Uptime (Core Identity)

The entire build's damage multiplier is gated behind maintaining Shadowform stacks:[6][1]
- Nether Step grants Shadowform stacks on cast
- Summon Laalish grants Shadowform stacks
- Terror Demon Metamorphosis grants Shadowform stacks
- Encircling Terror Dread Claws consumes Shadowform stacks for the linked AoE ring
- Mastermind Shard passive: Abyss Skills deal x30% increased damage while Shadowform is active, plus 5% Movement Speed per stack[1]

### Resource Loop

- **Wrath:** Generated by Hellion Sting (Eviscerate), Command Fallen (Fallen Rush), Ring of Starless Skies, Dominion passive, Wrath Regeneration affixes, and Unbound passive[6]
- **Dominance:** Generated by Rampage recasts and passive Dominance nodes; consumed by summoning Greater Demons[2]

### Blasphemous Fragment (Second Shard Slot)

Equipping both Mastermind Shard and Blasphemous Fragment is the endgame configuration. Blasphemous Fragment causes Recast Skills to Hex enemies on hit and increases damage they take from Abyss + Demonology Skills by x20%. Command Fallen (Fallen Rush) and Abyssal Titan Rampage both proc Hex via this fragment.[6][1]

### Berú of Horazon's Chains (Set Charm - Endgame)

The 5-piece Berú of Horazon's Chains set is the primary endgame Talisman set. When casting Summon Laalish, it automatically summons all 4 Shard Greater Demons for 10 seconds, providing x200% more damage and 34% Damage Reduction. Total Greater Demon count at full gear: 4 Shard Demons + 5 Profane Sentinels + 1 Abyssal Titan Rampage + 1 Sigil of Summons summon = 11 Greater Demons, producing approximately a 15x damage multiplier from the set bonus.[6]

The objective of SFT is to maximize the likelihood of the target response given the prompt, utilizing a cross-entropy loss function on the token sequence :$$L_{SFT}(\pi_{\theta}) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} \log(\pi_{\theta}(y_{i} | x, y_{1}, \dots, y_{i-1}))$$In this formulation, $x$ represents the prompt, $y$ is the sequence of $n$ tokens in the response, and $\pi_{\theta}$ is the policy being optimized. While SFT effectively teaches the model the structural and tonal expectations of dialogue, it is limited by the "alignment tax", a regression in performance on some academic benchmarks, and a tendency to mimic the noise or biases present in the demonstration data. Data quality is the dominant factor in this stage; empirical evidence suggests that a relatively small set of 10,000 meticulously curated examples can outperform significantly larger, noisier datasets.Preference Alignment and Reinforcement Learning from Human FeedbackThe limitations of SFT necessitated the development of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), a three-stage process designed to align model behavior with nuanced human preferences. Following SFT, a reward model (RM) is trained to act as a scalar scoring function, predicting which of two or more responses a human labeler would prefer. This involves the collection of comparison data, where labelers rank responses based on criteria like helpfulness, truthfulness, and safety.The final stage of RLHF involves optimizing the policy model using the reward model's signals, typically via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). PPO enables the model to maximize rewards while remaining constrained by a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty relative to the original SFT model, ensuring the model does not deviate too far into "reward hacking" behaviors. The complexity of this pipeline is significant, often requiring the simultaneous management of four distinct model instances: the actor (policy), the critic (value), the reward model, and the frozen reference model.Direct Preference Optimization and the Shift to Implicit RewardsTo address the engineering complexity and instability of RLHF, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) was introduced as an alternative that avoids explicit reward modeling. DPO reparameterizes the reward function such that the policy can be learned directly from preference data. The loss function for DPO is defined as :$$L_{DPO}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y^{+}, y^{-})} \left[ \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{+} | x)}{\pi_{ref}(y^{+} | x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{-} | x)}{\pi_{ref}(y^{-} | x)} \right) \right]$$where $y^{+}$ and $y^{-}$ represent the preferred and rejected responses, respectively, $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function, and $\beta$ controls the strength of the KL regularization. DPO is celebrated for its computational efficiency and stability, making it a preferred choice for enterprise fine-tuning where resources are constrained. Prefix caching extends this concept across multiple requests by identifying and storing the KV cache blocks of common prompt segments, such as system instructions or shared RAG context.Modern frameworks like vLLM implement hash-based prefix caching, where blocks are uniquely identified by a hash of the token sequence they contain. This allows for the skipping of redundant computations in multi-turn dialogues and agentic workflows, provided the prefix is exactly identical. For specialized applications like recommendation agents, beyond-prefix reuse architectures (e.g., RcLLM) decompose prompts into non-contiguous reusable blocks, such as item descriptions and user history segments, managed through distributed storage and similarity-aware placement algorithms.Advanced Masking and Bidirectional ContextualizationWhile decoder-only models typically use causal masks to enforce unidirectional attention, this can limit the model's ability to exploit historical context fully. IndicIFEval evaluates adherence across 14 Indic languages, demonstrating that machine-translation-based benchmarks often fail to capture the morphological richness and script systems of low-resource languages, leading to invalid constraints. Similarly, multimodal evaluations such as IFEval-Audio analyze whether models can maintain formatting and content boundaries when the instruction is derived from audio input. These benchmarks reveal that instruction-following capability often deteriorates significantly after alignment with non-text modalities.Agentic Logic and the Dynamics of Self-CorrectionThe pinnacle of instruction following is the emergence of agentic behavior, where models move beyond pattern completion to proactive problem-solving. This involves the development of iterative self-correction, where the model reviews and revises its own outputs based on internal or external feedback.The Control Theory of Self-CorrectionIterative self-correction can be modeled as a closed-loop feedback-control problem. The system's reliability is defined by the Error Introduction Rate (EIR) and the Error Correction Rate (ECR). Analysis of leading models shows a sharp threshold: only models with an EIR $\lesssim 0.5\%$ (e.g., o3-mini, Claude Opus 4.6) consistently benefit from self-correction; others, including GPT-5 in certain configurations, may degrade through overcorrection or the compounding of errors.This "accuracy-correction paradox" suggests that models with higher initial accuracy may have less room for improvement through intrinsic refinement, and the computational cost of additional correction steps often outweighs the marginal gains in accuracy. Benchmarks like CorrectBench have shown that while mixture-of-strategies (combining intrinsic and external correction) can improve accuracy on complex mathematical and coding tasks, a simple "thinking" trace or chain-of-thought baseline often remains the most efficient configuration.Rationale versus Instruction in Cognitive ModelingA striking finding in agentic cognitive modeling is the "compliance gap" between explicit instructions and logical rationales. Models exhibit higher adherence and superior response quality when a prompt includes the why (rationale) behind a task, rather than just the what (instruction). For example, in software engineering tasks, providing a rationale for adopting Test-Driven Development (TDD) led to the spontaneous generation of end-to-end tests, whereas providing the instruction alone resulted in zero compliance. This implies that for autonomous agents, design intent is a more robust driver of behavior than procedural commands.Conclusions and Future OutlookThe landscape of instruction following in large language models has matured into a sophisticated ecosystem where architectural efficiency, alignment methodologies, and rigorous verification frameworks converge. The resolution of the "instruction gap" remains the primary challenge for enterprise deployment, necessitating the development of models that can prioritize multi-faceted requirements within massive context windows. As reinforcement learning methodologies transition from human-centric to AI-centric feedback loops, the scalability of alignment will likely increase, though the need for human-grounded values in the "constitution" remains critical for safety and ethical coherence.The emergence of "thinking" models and agentic architectures signifies the beginning of a new era where models do not merely follow instructions but proactively clarify intent, resolve ambiguity, and self-correct through logical traces. The future of the field will likely be defined by the integration of these reasoning capabilities with inference-time scaling and beyond-prefix caching, creating a generation of AI systems that are as efficient in their execution as they are precise in their adherence to human intent. The ultimate goal is the creation of systems where the cognitive modeling path is indistinguishable from human logical processing, ensuring that instruction following is not a mechanical adherence to keywords but a profound understanding of semantic rationale.
